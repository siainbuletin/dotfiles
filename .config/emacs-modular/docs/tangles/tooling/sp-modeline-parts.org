Literate source for =libraries/modules/tooling/sp-modeline-parts.el=.

- Every line of the module lives in the =emacs-lisp= blocks below, in order.
- The =:tangle= path in the =#+PROPERTY= line at the top says where they are written.
- To tangle, see [[file:../../../README.org::*Tangling the literate files][Tangling the literate files]] in the README.

This module holds the segments of the mode line at the bottom of each window. =sp-lambda-modeline= and =sp-prot-modeline= both use it, so the two look and behave the same. Nothing happens until one of them calls =sp-modeline-parts-install=.

The layout is inspired by [[https://github.com/seagle0128/doom-modeline][doom-modeline]]: a few coloured boxes that fill the whole height of the line, a window number, a position that depends on the kind of buffer, and segments that disappear in narrow windows. The line is cut in a left and a right part by [[https://github.com/rougier/mode-line-maker][mode-line-maker]] (Nicolas Rougier), which aligns the two parts and truncates them when the window is too small.

* The new order

From left to right:

1. *The number of the window* (1, 2, 3... in the order of =window-list=, starting at the top left).
2. *The major mode*, as a nerd-icons icon (or its name when there is no icon) in a coloured box.
3. *The position*: the number of words in buffers of text modes (Org, Markdown, LaTeX, Typst and any mode listed in =sp-modeline-parts-word-count-modes=), and line and column in every other buffer.
4. *Keycast*: the key you used last in a coloured box and the command it ran. The last command stays on screen until the next one, and the segment has a fixed width (=sp-modeline-parts-keycast-width=), so the rest of the line does not move when it changes. Only the selected window shows keys; the other windows keep the empty space.
5. At the right: the *task being clocked* (see below), the tag emoji of =sp-tag-emoji= and the version control branch.

The count of minor modes was removed: it was not helpful, and its icon (a Material Design glyph, code point =0f0431=) showed as a square with the code in fonts that lack the Material Design set of Nerd Fonts. The minor modes can be listed with =C-h m= (=describe-mode=); click on the major mode box for the same.

The name of the buffer is not in this line: it is in the header line (=sp-nano-style=), together with the clock.

* Left and right parts: mode-line-maker

=mode-line-maker= builds the line from two lists of constructs, the left one and the right one. It formats both, measures them, truncates the left part (see =mode-line-maker-truncation-rule=: =left=, =right= or =both=) when they do not fit, and puts a space between them with an exact =:align-to= to the right edge. The extent of the line is =mode-line-maker-alignment=, a pair for the left and right ends: =window= (the whole window, the default), =fringe=, =margin= or =text=. For example =(setq mode-line-maker-alignment '(text . text))= lines the mode line up with the text. The package is installed with =use-package= from its repository, and the line falls back to a plain =:align-to= space if it is missing.

The left list is the segments in =sp-modeline-parts-left= and the right list those of =sp-modeline-parts-right=, so changing the order or the sides of a segment is changing those two lists. The README of mode-line-maker warns that its line is two to three times slower to draw than a plain one, because everything is computed on each redisplay.

* The task being clocked

When an Org entry is clocked in (=org-clock-in=, =C-c C-x C-i=, or from the agenda), the line shows a box with its todo keyword, then its heading (cut to =sp-modeline-parts-clock-width= columns) and the time clocked so far (=1:25=). Nothing is shown when no clock is running. Click on it with =mouse-1= to jump to the entry (=org-clock-goto=), with =mouse-3= to open the agenda (=org-agenda=).

Three things are wired to the clock:

- *The keyword.* On clock-in the entry is switched to =sp-modeline-parts-clock-keyword= (=PROG= by default, which is in =sp-org-keywords=; use any keyword of your sequences, or =nil= to leave the state alone). This is done through =org-clock-in-switch-to-state=, and only for an entry that has an open todo keyword already: a note with no keyword stays as it is. If you have set =org-clock-in-switch-to-state= yourself, it is not touched.
- *The arrow.* The clocked heading is flagged with an arrow in the fringe, after the =org-clock-flag-active-task= snippet that inspired it: the position is =overlay-arrow-position= of the buffer, set from =org-clock-in-hook= and removed from =org-clock-out-hook=. Turn it off with =sp-modeline-parts-clock-arrow=.
- *The box.* Its colour is =sp-modeline-parts-accent-4= (the =accent-3= of the =sp= themes, the warning colour in other themes). Remove =clock= from =sp-modeline-parts-boxed= to draw it as plain text.

* The boxes and their colours

The boxes take their colours from the accents of the theme in use:

- With the =sp-dark= and =sp-light= themes (=sp-theme-engine=), from =accent-1= (window number), =accent-2= (major mode), the theme's =cyan= (keycast) and =accent-3= (clock). They follow =sp-theme-set-color=.
- With any other theme, from the foreground of =font-lock-keyword-face=, =font-lock-function-name-face= and =font-lock-string-face=.

The text of a box is the background colour of the theme. The faces are =sp-modeline-parts-accent-1= to =-4=. In the windows that are not selected the boxes are not drawn: the text is muted (=shadow=). The colours are set again after every theme load (=sp-after-load-theme-hook=).

The faces are defined with a fallback that works in any theme. =sp-modeline-parts-update-accents= then gives them the accent colours of the theme in use.

(defun sp-modeline-parts--accent (n)
  "Return the colour of accent N (1 to 3) of the theme in use."
  (or (and (fboundp 'sp-theme-color)
           (seq-some (lambda (theme) (get theme 'sp-theme-flavor))
                     custom-enabled-themes)
           (ignore-errors
             (sp-theme-color (pcase n (1 'accent-1) (2 'accent-2) (3 'cyan) (_ 'accent-3)))))
      (face-foreground (pcase n
                         (1 'font-lock-keyword-face)
                         (2 'font-lock-function-name-face)
                         (3 'font-lock-string-face)
                         (_ 'font-lock-warning-face))
                       nil t)))

(defun sp-modeline-parts-update-accents (&rest _)
  "Set the box faces from the accents of the current theme."
  (let ((bg (face-background 'default nil t)))
    (dolist (n '(1 2 3 4))
      (let ((color (sp-modeline-parts--accent n))
            (face (intern (format "sp-modeline-parts-accent-%d" n))))
        (when (and (stringp color) (stringp bg))
          (set-face-attribute face nil
                              :inverse-video nil :weight 'bold
                              :foreground bg :background color))))
    (when (facep 'mode-line-maker-padding-face)
      (set-face-attribute 'mode-line-maker-padding-face nil
                          :foreground (face-foreground 'default nil t)
                          :background bg))
    (force-mode-line-update t)))
#+end_src

** Helpers

A box is the text padded with a space on each side, in the accent face for the selected window and muted in the others.

(defun sp-modeline-parts--narrow-p ()

(defun sp-modeline-parts--box (segment text face &rest props)
  "Return TEXT for SEGMENT, in a box of FACE when SEGMENT is boxed and selected.

  (let* ((selected (mode-line-window-selected-p))
         (boxed (memq segment sp-modeline-parts-boxed))
         (text (concat " " text " ")))
    (add-face-text-property 0 (length text)

                                  (selected 'bold)
                                  (t 'shadow))
                            nil text)

** Window number, major mode, position

The number is the place of the window among the windows of its frame (=window-list=, from the top left). The major mode is its nerd-icons icon; the icon keeps the font of the icon face and takes the colours of the box. The position is the word count in text modes and line and column in the others. The word count is cached: it is counted again only when the buffer has changed and at most twice a second, so typing in a long note does not slow down.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp -i
(defun sp-modeline-parts-window-number ()
  "Return the number of the window being displayed."
  (let* ((window (selected-window))
         (frame (window-frame window))
         (windows (window-list frame 'no-minibuf (frame-first-window frame)))
         (number (1+ (or (cl-position window windows) 0))))
    (sp-modeline-parts--box 'window-number (number-to-string number)
                            'sp-modeline-parts-accent-1
                            'help-echo (format "Window %d" number))))

(defun sp-modeline-parts--mode-icon ()
  "Return the nerd-icons icon of the major mode, or nil."
  (when (and sp-modeline-parts-mode-icon (require 'nerd-icons nil t))
    (let ((icon (nerd-icons-icon-for-mode major-mode)))
      (and (stringp icon) (not (string-empty-p icon)) (copy-sequence icon)))))

(defun sp-modeline-parts-mode ()
  "Return the major mode box."
  (let* ((name (format-mode-line mode-name))
         (icon (sp-modeline-parts--mode-icon)))
    (sp-modeline-parts--box
     'mode (or icon name) 'sp-modeline-parts-accent-2
     'help-echo (format "%s (`%s')\nmouse-1: describe-mode" name major-mode)
     'mouse-face 'mode-line-highlight
     'local-map (let ((map (make-sparse-keymap)))
                  (define-key map [mode-line down-mouse-1] #'describe-mode)
                  map))))

(defvar-local sp-modeline-parts--word-count nil
  "List of the modified tick, the time and the count last computed.")

(defun sp-modeline-parts--words ()
  "Return the number of words in the buffer, cached, or nil if it is too long."
  (let ((tick (buffer-chars-modified-tick))
        (now (float-time)))
    (if (and sp-modeline-parts--word-count
             (or (eq (nth 0 sp-modeline-parts--word-count) tick)
                 (< (- now (nth 1 sp-modeline-parts--word-count)) 0.5)))
        (nth 2 sp-modeline-parts--word-count)
      (let ((count (and (<= (buffer-size) sp-modeline-parts-word-count-limit)
                        (count-words (point-min) (point-max)))))
        (setq sp-modeline-parts--word-count (list tick now count))
        count))))

(defun sp-modeline-parts-position ()
  "Return the word count in text buffers, else line and column."
  (propertize
   (if (apply #'derived-mode-p sp-modeline-parts-word-count-modes)
       (let ((words (sp-modeline-parts--words)))
         (format " %s words " (or words "~")))
     (format " %d:%d " (line-number-at-pos) (current-column)))
   'face (if (mode-line-window-selected-p) nil 'shadow)))
#+end_src

** Keycast

keycast puts its segment into =mode-line-format= after a given element and refuses to start when it cannot find it. An empty anchor variable is added to the end of the format and keycast is told to insert its segment after it. That segment shows nothing (its window predicate is =ignore=): keycast is only used to follow the commands. The slot is drawn here with =keycast-format=, which gives the key (=%K=) and the command (=%C=).

The last key and command are kept in two variables, so they stay on screen after the command is over. The slot is padded or cut to =sp-modeline-parts-keycast-width= columns.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp -i
(defvar sp-modeline-parts-keycast-anchor ""
  "Empty element of `mode-line-format' that keycast inserts its segment after.")
(put 'sp-modeline-parts-keycast-anchor 'risky-local-variable t)

(defvar sp-modeline-parts--last-key ""
  "The last key keycast saw.")

(defvar sp-modeline-parts--last-command ""
  "The command of `sp-modeline-parts--last-key'.")

(defun sp-modeline-parts--keycast-remember ()
  "Save the key and command of keycast when the selected window shows a new one."
  (when (and (bound-and-true-p keycast-mode-line-mode)
             (mode-line-window-selected-p))
    (let ((key (ignore-errors (keycast-format "%K")))
          (command (ignore-errors (keycast-format "%C"))))
      (when (and (stringp key) (not (string-blank-p key)))
        (setq sp-modeline-parts--last-key (string-trim (substring-no-properties key))
              sp-modeline-parts--last-command
              (string-trim (substring-no-properties (or command ""))))))))

(defun sp-modeline-parts-keycast ()
  "Return the keycast slot: the last key in a box and its command."
  (when sp-modeline-parts-keycast
    (sp-modeline-parts--keycast-remember)
    (if (sp-modeline-parts--narrow-p)
        ""
      (let* ((selected (mode-line-window-selected-p))
             (key (if (string-empty-p sp-modeline-parts--last-key)
                      ""
                    (sp-modeline-parts--box 'keycast sp-modeline-parts--last-key
                                            'sp-modeline-parts-accent-3)))
             (command (if (string-empty-p sp-modeline-parts--last-command)
                          ""
                        (propertize (concat " " sp-modeline-parts--last-command)
                                    'face (if selected nil 'shadow)))))
        (truncate-string-to-width (concat key command)
                                  sp-modeline-parts-keycast-width 0 ?\s "…")))))

(defun sp-modeline-parts--start-keycast ()
  "Make keycast follow the commands, in the mode line only."
  (when (and sp-modeline-parts-keycast (require 'keycast nil t))
    (when (bound-and-true-p keycast-header-line-mode)
      (keycast-header-line-mode -1))
    (setq keycast-mode-line-insert-after 'sp-modeline-parts-keycast-anchor
          keycast-mode-line-window-predicate #'ignore
          keycast-mode-line-remove-tail-elements nil)
    (dolist (input '(self-insert-command org-self-insert-command))
      (add-to-list 'keycast-substitute-alist `(,input "." "Typing…")))
    (dolist (event '("<mouse-event>" "<mouse-movement>" "<mouse-2>" "<drag-mouse-1>"
                     "<wheel-up>" "<wheel-down>" "<double-wheel-up>" "<double-wheel-down>"
                     "<triple-wheel-up>" "<triple-wheel-down>" "<wheel-left>" "<wheel-right>"
                     handle-select-window mouse-set-point mouse-drag-region))
      (add-to-list 'keycast-substitute-alist `(,event nil nil)))
    (when (listp (default-value 'mode-line-format))
      (unless (memq 'sp-modeline-parts-keycast-anchor
                    (default-value 'mode-line-format))
        (setq-default mode-line-format
                      (append (default-value 'mode-line-format)
                              '(sp-modeline-parts-keycast-anchor)))))
    (unless (bound-and-true-p keycast-mode-line-mode)
      (keycast-mode-line-mode 1))))

** The task being clocked

=sp-modeline-parts-clock= shows the todo keyword (in a box), the heading and the time of the running clock, or nothing when no clock runs. The keyword is read from the clocked entry. The heading is =org-clock-heading=, which Org keeps without keyword and tags.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp -i
(defvar sp-modeline-parts-clock-map
  (let ((map (make-sparse-keymap)))
    (define-key map [mode-line mouse-1] #'org-clock-goto)
    (define-key map [mode-line mouse-3] #'org-agenda)
    map)
  "Keymap of the clocked task: mouse-1 goes to the entry, mouse-3 opens the agenda.")

(defun sp-modeline-parts--clock-state ()
  "Return the todo keyword of the clocked entry, or nil."
  (when (and (markerp org-clock-marker) (marker-buffer org-clock-marker))
    (ignore-errors
      (with-current-buffer (marker-buffer org-clock-marker)
        (save-excursion
          (goto-char org-clock-marker)
          (org-back-to-heading t)
          (org-get-todo-state))))))

(defun sp-modeline-parts-clock ()
  "Return the clocked task: keyword in a box, heading and time, or nil."
  (when (and (featurep 'org-clock) (org-clocking-p))
    (let* ((selected (mode-line-window-selected-p))
           (state (or (sp-modeline-parts--clock-state) "CLOCK"))
           (heading (truncate-string-to-width
                     (substring-no-properties (or org-clock-heading ""))
                     sp-modeline-parts-clock-width nil nil "…"))
           (time (org-duration-from-minutes (org-clock-get-clocked-time))))
      (propertize
       (concat (sp-modeline-parts--box 'clock state 'sp-modeline-parts-accent-4)
               (propertize (format " %s %s " heading time)
                           'face (if selected nil 'shadow)))
       'mouse-face 'mode-line-highlight
       'help-echo (format "Clocked: %s (%s)\nmouse-1: go to the entry\nmouse-3: org-agenda"
                          heading time)
       'local-map sp-modeline-parts-clock-map))))
#+end_src

*** Keyword and arrow

On clock-in the entry gets the keyword =sp-modeline-parts-clock-keyword=, when it has an open todo keyword and the keyword is one of the buffer's. The heading is flagged with the arrow of =overlay-arrow-position= in the buffer of the entry, and unflagged on clock-out.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp -i
(defun sp-modeline-parts-clock-switch-state ()
  "Return the keyword to switch the entry being clocked in to, or nil.
For `org-clock-in-switch-to-state'; point is on the heading."
  (when (and sp-modeline-parts-clock-keyword
             (member sp-modeline-parts-clock-keyword org-todo-keywords-1))
    (let ((state (org-get-todo-state)))
      (and state
           (not (member state org-done-keywords))
           sp-modeline-parts-clock-keyword))))

(defun sp-modeline-parts-clock-flag ()
  "Flag the clocked-in heading with a fringe arrow."
  (when (and sp-modeline-parts-clock-arrow
             (markerp org-clock-marker) (marker-buffer org-clock-marker))
    (with-current-buffer (marker-buffer org-clock-marker)
      (save-excursion
        (goto-char org-clock-marker)
        (org-back-to-heading t)
        (setq-local overlay-arrow-position (copy-marker (point)))))))

(defun sp-modeline-parts-clock-unflag ()
  "Remove the fringe arrow from the buffers of the clocked entries."
  (dolist (marker (list org-clock-marker (car org-clock-history)))
    (when (and (markerp marker) (marker-buffer marker))
      (with-current-buffer (marker-buffer marker)
        (kill-local-variable 'overlay-arrow-position)))))

(defun sp-modeline-parts--clock-install ()
  "Wire the keyword switch and the arrow to Org's clock."
  (unless org-clock-in-switch-to-state
    (setq org-clock-in-switch-to-state #'sp-modeline-parts-clock-switch-state))
  (add-hook 'org-clock-in-hook #'sp-modeline-parts-clock-flag)
  (add-hook 'org-clock-out-hook #'sp-modeline-parts-clock-unflag))

(defun sp-modeline-parts--clock-uninstall ()
  "Undo `sp-modeline-parts--clock-install'."

** Composing the line

=sp-modeline-parts-compose= returns the whole line. The left and right lists are =:eval= constructs, and =mode-line-maker--make= aligns them and cuts the left one if they do not fit; its result is a list of strings, which is joined. Without mode-line-maker the right part is pushed to the edge with an =:align-to= space. =sp-lambda-modeline= calls it; =sp-prot-modeline= puts the same lists in =mode-line-maker= itself.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp -i
(defun sp-modeline-parts--join (segments)
  "Return the strings of SEGMENTS (symbols) joined by a space."
  (mapconcat #'identity
             (delq nil
                   (mapcar (lambda (segment)
                             (let ((text (funcall (intern (format "sp-modeline-parts-%s" segment)))))
                               (and (stringp text) (not (string-empty-p text)) text)))
                           segments))
             " "))

(defun sp-modeline-parts-compose ()
  "Return the whole line: the left segments, a gap, and the right ones."
  (if (and (require 'mode-line-maker nil t) (fboundp 'mode-line-maker--make))
      (apply #'concat
             (mode-line-maker--make
              '((:eval (sp-modeline-parts--join sp-modeline-parts-left)))
              '((:eval (sp-modeline-parts--join sp-modeline-parts-right)))))
    (let ((left (sp-modeline-parts--join sp-modeline-parts-left))
          (right (concat (sp-modeline-parts--join sp-modeline-parts-right) " ")))
      (concat left
              (propertize " " 'display
                          `(space :align-to (- right ,(string-width right))))
              right))))
#+end_src

** Constructs for Prot's line

A segment as a mode line construct is a variable holding an =:eval= form, marked as safe to evaluate. These are what =sp-prot-modeline-format= lists.

** Install

=sp-modeline-parts-install= starts keycast, wires the clock and sets the colours of the boxes; the colours are set again after each theme load, after the other hooks (depth 90). It does not touch =mode-line-format=: that is the job of the modeline module that calls it, and it must call this /after/ it has set its format, because keycast edits the format.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp -i
(defun sp-modeline-parts-install ()
  "Set the box colours and start keycast.  Call after setting `mode-line-format'."
  (interactive)
  (sp-modeline-parts-update-accents)
  (add-hook 'sp-after-load-theme-hook #'sp-modeline-parts-update-accents 90)
  (sp-modeline-parts--start-keycast)
  (with-eval-after-load 'org-clock (sp-modeline-parts--clock-install))
  (force-mode-line-update t))

(defun sp-modeline-parts-uninstall ()
  "Stop updating the box colours, and stop keycast in the mode line."
  (interactive)
  (remove-hook 'sp-after-load-theme-hook #'sp-modeline-parts-update-accents)
  (when (featurep 'org-clock) (sp-modeline-parts--clock-uninstall))
  (when (bound-and-true-p keycast-mode-line-mode)
    (keycast-mode-line-mode -1)))
#+end_src
