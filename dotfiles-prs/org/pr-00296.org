**** What

*Emacs (Linux/Windows, =sp-text-manipulation=)*

- =s-c= is translated to =C-c= and =s-x= to =C-x= (=key-translation-map=), so Super carries the prefixes: =s-c C-c= = =C-c C-c=, =s-x C-f= = =C-x C-f=.
- A typed =C-c= / =C-x= that starts a key sequence becomes copy / cut (private events =<sp-copy>=, =<sp-cut>=). Inside a sequence they keep their stock meaning. This replaces the =C-S-c= / =C-S-x= from #294.
- On Windows =w32-lwindow-modifier= / =w32-rwindow-modifier= are =super=.
- Mac is unchanged.

*i3 and sway (options for freeing Super)*

- Chosen: Caps Lock becomes the Level 3 shift (=lv3:caps_switch=, Mod5) and =$mod= is =Mod5=. Nothing binds Mod4 any more, so the Windows key reaches Emacs. Same physical layer key as AeroSpace on the Mac.
- Other options, not taken: =caps:hyper= (Hyper often shares Mod4 in X, so it may not free Super); Alt as =$mod= (clashes with Meta, which Sia reserved for Emacs); Ctrl+Alt chords (awkward, and Ctrl+Alt is used by terminals/Emacs).
- Caveat of the choice: Caps+letter yields AltGr-level characters on layouts that have them, but the WM grabs bound combinations first.

*Docs*

- =sp-keys.org=: new section "Replaced prefixes and chords" with a table of stock key vs Mac vs Linux/Windows, and how to look up a key (=C-h k=, =C-h w=).
- Top-level =README.org=: note that most keys are bound differently, pointing to that table.
- =sp-text-manipulation.org= and =global-keymap-namespaces.org= updated, with the caveats (Windows takes =Win+C= / =Win+X=; needs a remap).
